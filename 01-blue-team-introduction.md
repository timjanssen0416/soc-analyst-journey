# Blue Team Introduction – SOC Analyst Level 1

## 🎯 Objective
This module introduces the role of a Junior SOC Analyst, the position of the SOC within a company, and the importance of the human factor in cyber security. The goal is to understand daily SOC responsibilities, team structures, and common attack vectors involving people.

---

## 👨‍💻 Junior SOC Analyst – Role Overview

As a **Junior SOC Analyst (Level 1)**, your primary responsibilities include:

- Monitoring and investigating security alerts
- Performing initial alert triage
- Escalating incidents to senior analysts when required
- Collaborating with other security and IT teams
- Continuously learning about new attack techniques and defenses

### Typical scenarios a Junior SOC Analyst may handle:
- Detecting and responding to phishing campaigns
- Investigating malware infections on endpoints
- Assisting during large-scale incidents such as ransomware attacks
- Supporting detection rule creation and security automation

This role requires both **technical skills** and **analytical thinking**, combined with strong communication.

---

## 🛡️ SOC Role Within the Blue Team

### Blue Team vs Other Security Teams

In larger organizations, security is divided into specialized teams:

- **Red Team** – Offensive security (penetration testing, ethical hacking)
- **GRC Team** – Governance, Risk, and Compliance (policies, regulations)
- **Blue Team** – Defensive security (SOC analysts, engineers, incident responders)

The **Blue Team** focuses on **detecting, investigating, and responding to threats**.

### SOC Structure
A typical SOC is structured as follows:

- **SOC L1 Analysts** – Triage alerts and handle initial investigations
- **SOC L2 Analysts** – Investigate advanced threats and complex incidents
- **SOC Engineers** – Maintain and configure security tools (SIEM, EDR)
- **SOC Manager** – Oversees operations and team performance

There are also more specialized roles such as:
- Digital Forensics Analyst
- Threat Intelligence Analyst
- Application Security Engineer
- AI Security Researcher

## 🏢 Internal SOC vs MSSP

| Aspect | Internal SOC | MSSP |
|------|-------------|------|
| Environment | One organization | Multiple customers |
| Pace | Generally calmer | High alert volume |
| Tools | Few tools, deep expertise | Many tools, broad knowledge |
| Incidents | Fewer large incidents | Frequent real-world attacks |

Both environments provide valuable experience, but MSSPs often offer faster exposure to diverse threats.

## 👤 Humans as Attack Vectors

### Why Attackers Target Humans
Humans are often the **weakest link** in cyber security. Instead of exploiting technical vulnerabilities, attackers manipulate people to gain access.

Common goals include:
- Stealing credentials
- Gaining network access
- Bypassing security controls
- Preparing for larger attacks

### Common Social Engineering Techniques
Social engineering attacks exploit human psychology and often rely on:

- **Trust** – Appearing legitimate
- **Emotion** – Urgency, fear, curiosity

Examples include:
- Phishing emails
- Malware disguised as legitimate software
- Impersonation of IT staff or executives
- Deepfake audio or video scams
- USB drop attacks and insider threats

## 🧠 Detection & Mitigation

### Detection (SOC Responsibility)
As a SOC analyst, your role is to:
- Detect suspicious behavior
- Investigate alerts and indicators
- Escalate confirmed threats
- Support incident response

### Mitigation (Preventive Measures)
While detection is critical, prevention helps reduce SOC workload. Effective mitigation strategies include:

- Anti-phishing solutions
- Endpoint protection (AV / EDR)
- Security awareness training
- Verification procedures for sensitive requests
- Phishing simulations

Even strong mitigation can be bypassed — which is why SOC monitoring remains essential.

---

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- SOC Analysts are defenders focused on detection and response
- Blue Teams protect organizations from real-world threats
- Humans are a major attack vector in cyber security
- Social engineering exploits trust and emotions
- Detection and mitigation must work together
