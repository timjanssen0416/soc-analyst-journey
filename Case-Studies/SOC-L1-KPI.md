# 📊 SOC Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

This section outlines the most important **SOC KPIs** used to measure detection efficiency, analyst performance, and overall security posture. As a SOC Level 1 Analyst, understanding and improving these metrics is a core responsibility.

---

## 🔑 Core SOC KPIs

### 1. Alerts Count
**Definition:**  
Total number of alerts generated by security tools (SIEM, EDR, IDS).

**Why it matters:**  
- Too many alerts → analyst fatigue & missed threats  
- Too few alerts → blind spots or logging failures  

**Healthy range:**  
- 5–30 alerts per L1 analyst per day

---

### 2. False Positive Rate (FPR)
**Formula:**  

**Why it matters:**  
- High FPR creates alert fatigue  
- Reduces analyst vigilance  

**Target:**  
- Below 80% (lower is better)

**L1 Analyst contribution:**  
- Correct classification of alerts  
- Clear reporting of noisy detections  
- Feedback to detection engineers

---

### 3. Alert Escalation Rate (AER)
**Formula:**  

**Why it matters:**  
- Shows L1 analyst experience and confidence  
- Too high → unnecessary L2 workload  
- Too low → risk of missed incidents  

**Target:**  
- < 50% (ideal < 20%)

---

### 4. Threat Detection Rate (TDR)
**Formula:**  

**Why it matters:**  
- Measures SOC effectiveness  
- Any missed threat can cause major impact  

**Target:**  
- 100%

---

## ⏱️ SLA-Based KPIs

### Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)
Time between the attack occurring and detection by SOC tools.

**Target:**  
- ≤ 5 minutes

---

### Mean Time to Acknowledge (MTTA)
Time before an analyst starts triaging the alert.

**Target:**  
- ≤ 10 minutes

---

### Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)
Time required to contain and remediate the incident.

**Target:**  
- ≤ 60 minutes

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaway
> KPIs do not only measure SOC performance — they guide analyst behavior, tool tuning, and operational maturity.
